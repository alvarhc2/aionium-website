01 — The constraint

Public models are capable. They are also elsewhere.

A hospital, a criminal defense firm, a bank, an asset manager, a defense supplier: the work product is the confidential matter. Patient records, case files, client books, and deal files sit under an NDA, an audit duty, or a security rule that forbids handing them to an outside vendor. The client usually asks before the regulator does.

The statutes still set the floor. GDPR governs personal data. The EU AI Act sets fines up to €35 million, or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, for prohibited practices, and NIS2 covers the resilience of essential systems. What they do not forgive is ungoverned data, a leaked file, or an answer nobody can trace.

  • Many subscriptions, used all day

    A subscription for every person adds up, and the people who use it all day add more. A fixed monthly fee on your own hardware can cost less than subscriptions plus extra usage.

  • A bill that follows the volume

    Long documents, repeated checks, and internal scripts sent through a metered service are charged per question. On your own machine, that cost is the hardware and the power.

  • Building the search yourselves

    A chat subscription is a place to talk, not a search of your own files that already knows who may see what. Paying engineers to build and keep that layer can cost more than a ready appliance.

  • The cost that arrives later

    The saving is not always a smaller invoice this month. It is the security rework, the client who objects, or the migration once sensitive work is tied to a vendor.

Aionium is built for that constraint. The intelligence moves in. The documents stay where they are.

02 — The platform

What runs inside the perimeter.

01

Air-gapped isolation

Execution stays on hardware you control. Queries, documents, and logs are kept off external APIs and third-party servers.

02

Identity-aware access

The appliance joins the directory you already run. A person retrieves only what their existing permissions allow, including inside search results and model context.

03

Auditable answers

A parser, a domain specialist, and an auditor work in sequence. Citations are attached at paragraph level. Claims that cannot be sourced are flagged before anyone sees them.

04

A cost that stays put

Continuous indexing and multi-step analysis are where metered token bills break. On-premise capacity is fixed, so the operational fee does not rise because a team asked more questions.

05

Ownership of the whole stack

Adapters trained on your terminology, embeddings, document indexes, and audit logs remain the organization’s property.

03 — The method

Three agents, then a check.

01

Document parser

Extracts and structures scans, PDFs, and office files, including difficult layouts, and prepares them for retrieval.

02

Domain specialist

A smaller model, adapted to your language, does the analysis. It stays inside the appliance with the documents it reads.

03

Auditor

Checks generated claims against your own index. Supported statements carry a source. Unsupported ones are marked as such.

Routine work
Classification, extraction, and search stay on smaller local models, where they are fast and inexpensive to run.
Multi-step analysis
Harder questions escalate to a larger model on the same appliance. The work does not leave the building to find more compute.

Lightweight adapters learn internal terms and document formats on site. Adaptation uses QLoRA, so base model weights stay frozen and the documents used for it never leave the premises.

05 — Deployments

Sized to the building.

Tier 2

On-prem server

A server in your own rack for ten to one hundred people. Organization-wide retrieval and knowledge work.

Tier 3

Enterprise cluster

Multiple nodes for a hundred people and beyond. Core operational workflows and the largest local models.
